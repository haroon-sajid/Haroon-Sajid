Section 1

What the client is really doing

This is not a normal automation project. The client is not automating an existing sales process. They are building a sales motion from zero in a new country, and the AI system is the sales team.

  • A specialty kitchen equipment brand entering Colombia through a regional distributor.
  • Current sales are very low: about 1 to 2 deals per year, closed by their distributor in Spain.
  • No CRM, no sending domains, no mailboxes, no email reputation. Everything starts from zero.
  • Target market: hotels, restaurants, catering, hospitals, event venues, dark kitchens and resorts. Around 100 channel partners plus 35,000 to 40,000 end market establishments.

Why this matters: the automation is the easy part. The hard parts are data quality, email deliverability and brand safety in a market where the client has no history yet.

Section 2

The system in plain words

Four agents, each doing one clear job, chained together so a lead moves from discovery to a booked meeting without manual handoff.

Agent 1

Scout

Watches news, public tenders and new construction announcements. Finds the right decision maker, then finds and verifies their email and LinkedIn profile.

Agent 2

Qualifier

Reads the prospect's website, scores the lead using BANT (budget, authority, need, timing). Only strong leads move forward.

Agent 3

Copywriter

Writes outreach tailored to each segment. A hospital gets a different message than a boutique hotel. Creates A and B versions for testing.

Agent 4

Virtual SDR

Sends sequences within safe limits, understands replies, answers questions, and always steers the conversation toward booking a meeting with the human sales team.

Scout→ Qualifier→ Copywriter→ Virtual SDR→ Booked Meeting

Every lead and every booked meeting lands in the CRM automatically.

Section 4

Honest risk assessment

These are the things that are harder than they look. Naming them now is what protects the project later.

1. Deliverability is the biggest risk, not the AI

Reaching 35,000 to 40,000 contacts means roughly 800 to 900 emails per day at peak. A safe limit is about 30 to 50 emails per day per mailbox. So the project needs around 15 to 30 mailboxes spread across 8 to 15 lookalike domains, and every domain needs 3 to 4 weeks of warm up before real sending. This infrastructure must start in month 1, not month 5. Health targets once live: bounce rate under 2 percent, and spam complaints under 0.1 percent as the working target. Google enforces a hard ceiling of 0.3 percent, and senders above it lose deliverability with no way to appeal, so the system will pause any campaign that approaches this line automatically.

2. Colombia specific realities

Contact data coverage in Latin America is weaker than in the US or Europe. Expect email find rates around 40 to 60 percent, not 80. Colombia also has a strict data protection law, Ley 1581, enforced by the SIC. Important detail: a named employee email like juan.perez at a company domain counts as protected personal data under the SIC position, and only generic addresses like info at a company domain sit outside the law. The SIC has also fined companies hundreds of millions of pesos for unsolicited commercial prospecting. This does not kill the project, but it must be treated as a managed legal risk, not a solved problem. The compliant setup: clear sender identity, an opt out in every message, removal requests honored within 15 business days, the exclusion registry checked before prospecting, and a first touch built around asking permission where possible. I recommend a short review by the client's local legal counsel before mass sending begins.

3. Scout is the hardest agent

There is no single database of new hotels and restaurants opening in Colombia. Scout will combine SECOP tenders, the RUES registry, news monitoring and targeted scraping. It works, but it is an ongoing data pipeline that needs maintenance, not a one time build. Around a third of the total build effort sits here.

4. LinkedIn automation carries account risk

Automated LinkedIn outreach goes against LinkedIn terms and accounts can get restricted. The safe path is dedicated seats, low daily limits, and written acceptance of this risk from the client before launch.

5. Fully autonomous from day one is dangerous

For a brand with no presence in Colombia, one bad automated email to the wrong person can hurt the brand before it even starts. My recommendation: the system runs with human approval gates for the first 4 to 6 weeks, then moves to full autonomy once reply quality is proven. Serious buyers respect this answer.

6. Success must mean booked meetings, not closed deals

The client currently closes 1 to 2 deals per year. The system's job is a full calendar of qualified meetings for the human sales team. Closing those deals stays a human job, and expectations should be set that way from the start.

7. The channel choice leaves out WhatsApp, and that is a real trade off

WhatsApp is the dominant business channel in Colombia. Around 92 percent of Colombian internet users are on it, and many small restaurants, caterers and dark kitchens have no corporate email at all. This means the roughly 100 channel partners are the strong email and LinkedIn targets, while a meaningful part of the 35,000 to 40,000 end market establishments will be hard to reach by email alone. My recommendation: keep version 1 as email plus LinkedIn as scoped, but plan WhatsApp Business API as a fast follow phase, used only after a prospect replies and opts in, never as a cold first touch. The client should approve this channel decision knowingly, not discover it in month 6.

Section 5

Is 9 months realistic?

Yes, 9 months is realistic. But only one part of the original plan must change: month 1 cannot be "build everything." Month 1 has to be foundation and infrastructure, because domain warm up cannot be skipped or rushed.

Months 1 to 2
FoundationDomains bought and warming, mailboxes ready, CRM and databases live, product knowledge base built in Spanish, Scout version 1 collecting leads.
Months 2 to 3
Agent build and pilotAll four agents connected end to end. Pilot launch to the roughly 100 channel partners with human approval on every message. This small group is the perfect low risk testing ground.
Months 4 to 8
Scaled prospectingMass outreach to the end market, rolled out region by region and segment by segment, volume increasing weekly while bounce and spam numbers stay inside the health targets.
Month 9
Optimization and proofTuning based on real meeting outcomes, final ROI report, and the plan for what comes next.

  • Meeting capacity of the human sales team, so the booking volume matches what they can handle.
  • Confirmation of the monthly tool budget range above.
  • Confirmation that the client owns all domains and tool accounts.

Bottom line. This project is very buildable, and 9 months is enough with the right phasing. My one condition is that we build it right, not fast. A system that lands in spam folders is worse than no system at all.
